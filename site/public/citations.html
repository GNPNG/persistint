Further reading

Social and relational aggression among girls

  • Björkqvist, K., Lagerspetz, K. M. J., & Kaukiainen, A. (1992). Do girls manipulate and boys fight? Developmental trends in regard to direct and indirect aggression. Aggressive Behavior, 18(2), 117–127. https://doi.org/10.1002/1098-2337(1992)18:2<117::AID-AB2480180205>3.0.CO;2-3 One of the first studies to show that girls tend to hurt each other indirectly, through manipulation and exclusion, rather than by fighting.
  • Crick, N. R., & Grotpeter, J. K. (1995). Relational aggression, gender, and social-psychological adjustment. Child Development, 66(3), 710–722. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8624.1995.tb00900.x The study that named “relational aggression”: harming someone by damaging their friendships. Girls in grades 3 to 6 did it more than boys.
  • Underwood, M. K. (2003). Social aggression among girls. The Guilford Press. http://openlibrary.org/books/OL8734420M/Social_Aggression_among_Girls_(Guilford_Series_On_Social_And_Emotional_Development) The main academic book on social aggression among girls: what it looks like, why it happens and what it does.
  • Vaillancourt, T. (2013). Do human females use indirect aggression as an intrasexual competition strategy? Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B, 368(1631), 20130080. https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2013.0080 A review of how women and girls compete with each other through gossip, exclusion and criticism of looks.

Cyberbullying and online gossip

  • García-Fernández, C. M., Moreno-Moya, M., Ortega-Ruiz, R., & Romera, E. M. (2022). Adolescent involvement in cybergossip: Influence on social adjustment, bullying and cyberbullying. The Spanish Journal of Psychology, 25, e6. https://doi.org/10.1017/SJP.2022.3 How gossip online feeds bullying among teenagers; girls were the most involved in cybergossip.
  • Jackson, M., Cassidy, W., & Brown, K. (2009). “you were born ugly and youl die ugly too”: Cyber-bullying as relational aggression. in education, 15(2). https://journals.uregina.ca/ineducation/article/view/57/539 A Canadian study of cyberbullying in grades 6 to 9, including attacks on looks; girls were more likely than boys to take part. Free to read.

Appearance and body-shaming

  • Vaillancourt, T., & Sharma, A. (2011). Intolerance of sexy peers: Intrasexual competition among women. Aggressive Behavior, 37(6), 569–577. https://doi.org/10.1002/ab.20413 An experiment in which women were more hostile toward, and more likely to shut out, a woman dressed to look attractive.

Coercive control and abuse in relationships between women

  • Porsch, L. M., Xu, M., Veldhuis, C. B., Bochicchio, L. A., Zollweg, S. S., & Hughes, T. L. (2023). Intimate partner violence among sexual minority women: A scoping review. Trauma, Violence, & Abuse, 24(5), 3014–3036. https://doi.org/10.1177/15248380221122815 A review of the research on partner abuse among lesbian, bisexual and other sexual minority women.
  • Renzetti, C. M. (1992). Violent betrayal: Partner abuse in lesbian relationships. SAGE Publications. https://doi.org/10.4135/9781483325767 The classic study of abuse by female partners in lesbian relationships.
  • Hilton, N. Z., Ham, E., Radatz, D. L., Smith, C. M., Snow, N., Wintermute, J., Jennings-Fitz-Gerald, E., Lee, J., & Patterson, S. (2024). Coercive control in 2SLGBTQQIA+ relationships: A scoping review. Trauma, Violence, & Abuse, 25(5), 3713–3728. https://doi.org/10.1177/15248380241257957 A review of coercive control outside the man-abusing-woman pattern, including in relationships between women.

Conflict between women at work

  • Sheppard, L. D., & Aquino, K. (2013). Much ado about nothing? Observers’ problematization of women’s same-sex conflict at work. Academy of Management Perspectives, 27(1), 52–62. https://doi.org/10.5465/amp.2012.0005 How co-workers judge a conflict between two women differently from the same conflict between men.
  • Sheppard, L. D., & Aquino, K. (2017). Sisters at arms: A theory of female same-sex conflict and its problematization in organizations. Journal of Management, 43(3), 691–715. https://doi.org/10.1177/0149206314539348 A theory of why conflict between women at work happens and why it is treated as a special problem.
