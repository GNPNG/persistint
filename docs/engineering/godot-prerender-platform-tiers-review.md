# Pre-rendering and platform tiers for the paper look: engineering review

Godot 4.6.3, Compatibility renderer, web first, tablets and phones next. Written 2026-10-24 against the repository at that date and the current Godot 4.x documentation and engine source (4.6 branch). Every external claim carries a source number; the Sources list at the end resolves them. Anything not verified is marked so.

## Summary

The owner's direction is to move compute out of the engine and into the assets: bake shadows, edges and other per-frame work offline, detect the platform at launch, load the asset set and switch on the rendering features that platform can carry, and test that degradation ourselves instead of trusting the engine's. The repository already does this twice: `tools/gen_paper_edges.py` bakes the paper border and drop shadow into sibling sheets (the C column of the paper_look scene, two draws a subject against ten for the engine-side B column), and the v0.2.1 hotfix baked the clone voices offline after removing real-time PitchShift and Distortion. The engine evidence supports baking where the renderer's cost is per pixel per light or per draw call (Light2D, shadows, outline copies, material swaps) and does not support baking where the engine's cost is one uniform (CanvasModulate is a single multiply in the fragment shader). Three repository facts shape the action points more than any documentation: the village scene contains no Light2D, CanvasModulate, occluder or particle node, so the Night frame-rate halving in ticket 89f cannot come from the lighting the ticket suspects; the project ships with Nearest filtering (hotfix 3008596) while 339 of 361 textures still generate mipmaps that Nearest never samples; and all 361 textures import as Lossless or Lossy with zero VRAM-compressed textures, so the export preset's texture-compression flags currently change nothing. The largest unmeasured quantity is the one the owner named: no frame time has been recorded on a tablet. The owner's caveat stands: what he sees on the tablet may be bugs, not engine cost, and the measurements below are designed to separate the two.

## 1. Where the Compatibility renderer spends 2D frame time

The Compatibility renderer is OpenGL 3.3 / ES 3.0 / WebGL 2 and is the only renderer on the web [1][9]. The docs describe it as "Low base cost, but high scaling cost" [9]. The GLES3 2D canvas renderer in the 4.6 source batches canvas items into instanced draws: each batch is one `glDrawElementsInstanced` [16]. Lighting in 4.x GL is per-instance data, not extra passes; the 3.x batching page still ranks first in web search, and the 4.x stable copy of that page returns 404, so the rules below come from the 4.6 source, not from any 4.x documentation page.

What starts a new batch in `rasterizer_canvas_gles3.cpp` [16]: a change of clip owner (`final_clip_owner`), of material, of texture filter, of texture repeat, of the lighting-disabled specialization, of blend mode or blend colour, of texture (`rect->texture != ... .tex`) or command type, of primitive point count, of the clip-ignore flag, and the instance buffer filling up (`max_instances_per_buffer`, 16384 [17]). Polygons and meshes never batch: "Polygon's can't be batched, so always create a new batch" and "Meshes can't be batched" [16]. A back-buffer copy or a CanvasGroup flushes everything pending before it [16]. Consequences for this project: every distinct texture is a batch boundary, so 71 y-sorted props over 17 textures interleaved with three NPC rig sheets and the player's layered sheets cannot merge into a few draws; a `ShaderMaterial` on one sprite (the B column's `paper_flat`, the tap `pixelate`) splits the batch on both sides; the `texture_filter` toggle in paper_look splits batches between nodes with different filters.

Modulate costs nothing extra. CanvasModulate is a uniform (`state_buffer.canvas_modulate`) [16] applied as `color *= canvas_modulation;` in the fragment shader [18]; a node's `final_modulate` is multiplied into per-instance data on the CPU [16]. Neither adds a draw or breaks a batch. The docs limit CanvasModulate to one per canvas, with CanvasLayers rendering independently [14].

Lights are the per-pixel cost. The fragment shader reads `light_count` from the instance flags ("Max 16 lights") and loops `for (uint i = 0u; i < MAX_LIGHTS_PER_ITEM; i++)`, reading the light texture per light with `textureLod(atlas_texture, ...)` and, when the light casts shadows, sampling the shadow atlas once (None), five times (PCF5) or thirteen times (PCF13) [18]; `MAX_LIGHTS_PER_ITEM = 16`, `DEFAULT_MAX_LIGHTS_PER_RENDER = 256` [17]. Shadows are rendered separately per light by `light_update_shadow()` drawing occluders into an atlas sized by `rendering/2d/shadow_atlas/size` (default 2048) [16][4]. The docs say larger lights "affect more pixels on screen", PCF13 "should only be used for a few lights at once", and "Additive sprites are much faster to render, since they don't need to go through a separate rendering pipeline" [6]. The source carries the comment "TODO: consider making lights a per-batch property and then baking light operations in the shader for better performance" [16], i.e. the lit path is acknowledged as unoptimised. The renderers page adds "Lights with shadows use a multi-pass approach and less accurate blending" [9]. None of this cost is paid in the village today because the scene has no lights; it is the cost the paper look would buy if it chose engine shadows.

Mobile GPUs: the performance docs say mobile GPUs "may struggle with scenes that run easily on desktop", are fill-rate bound ("especially on mobile, where fill rate is very expensive"), that "Transparent objects are also particularly bad for fill rate", that "Reading textures is also expensive in terms of power usage, which is a big issue on mobiles", and recommend "the simplest possible shaders" [8]. The 2D path draws every sprite as a transparent quad, so the lever is the number and size of textured quads per pixel of screen, which y-sorted overlap and padded edge sheets both raise.

Texture filtering and mipmaps: `TEXTURE_FILTER_NEAREST` "makes the texture look pixelated from up close, and grainy from a distance (due to mipmaps not being sampled)"; `TEXTURE_FILTER_LINEAR_WITH_MIPMAPS` "smooth from up close, and smooth from a distance" and is recommended "for non-pixel art textures that may be viewed at a low scale" [20]. Mipmap generation "increases memory use by roughly 33%" and in 2D should be enabled only when the camera zooms out [5]. The shipped state (Nearest, mipmaps generated on 339 textures) pays the 33% and samples none of it, and samples heavily minified sources point by point: a 480 px tall rig frame shown at 52 world px is minified 9.2x before any screen scaling.

Texture compression per platform: Lossless is the 2D default and "uses considerably more video memory"; Lossy has the same VRAM cost as Lossless and a smaller file; VRAM Compressed cuts memory "roughly 4 to 6 times" but the docs say to avoid it in 2D because "artifacts are noticeable" [5]. VRAM-compressed formats are S3TC/BPTC on desktop and ETC2/ASTC on mobile and web, with High Quality disabled in Compatibility [5]; the importer "always imports the format the host platform needs" and the project settings `rendering/textures/vram_compression/import_etc2_astc` / `import_s3tc_bptc` add the other family (apply by deleting `.godot/imported/`) [4]; the web preset's `for_desktop` / `for_mobile` select which families ship [1]. The engine picks ETC2/ASTC on ARM and S3TC/BPTC on x86 hosts [23]. With zero VRAM-compressed textures in this project, none of these switches currently has an effect. Lossless textures are stored as WebP unless `rendering/textures/lossless_compression/force_png` is set [4]; load time for Lossless and Lossy is rated slow against VRAM Compressed's fast [5], which matters on a tablet at scene load. Bytes per pixel for the formula in section 4: RGBA8 is four 8-bit components [50]; DXT1 stores "16 input pixels in 64 bits" and DXT5 "16 input pixels into 128 bits" [47]; ETC2 RGB is 64 bits per 4x4 block and ETC2 RGBA 128 bits per block, mandatory in OpenGL ES 3.0 [49]; ASTC uses 128-bit blocks from 4x4 to 12x12, "8 bits per texel down to 0.89 bits per texel" [48].

TileMapLayer: "A quadrant is a group of tiles to be drawn together on a single canvas item" (default 16x16 cells), and "The quadrant size does not apply on a Y-sorted TileMapLayer, as tiles are grouped by Y position instead" [19]. The village's six layers are not themselves y-sorted (no `y_sort_enabled` on them; they sit at negative z_index), so they draw as quadrant items, a few per layer at 40x38 cells.

Large textures: the WebGL2 `MAX_TEXTURE_SIZE` survey reports 100% of contexts at >= 4096 overall, Android >= 8192 at 74% and >= 16384 at 3%, iOS >= 8192 at 100% [36]. Godot's own ceiling is 16384x16384 "due to graphics hardware limitations" [50]. The `size_limit` import option exists to avoid "textures not displaying on mobile/web platforms" [5]. The tile atlas already respects 4096 (commit 36cb6ae).

## 2. Mechanisms for platform-aware tiers at launch

Feature tags [2][10][22][23]: `OS.get_name()` returns "Web" on web and "iOS", "Android" and so on natively [10]. `OS.has_feature()` is case-sensitive; on web the engine adds one of `web_android`, `web_ios`, `web_linuxbsd`, `web_macos`, `web_windows` [10], decided in JavaScript (`godot_js_os_has_feature`) [22]. The default tags include `web`, `mobile`, `pc`, `threads` / `nothreads`, `wasm32`, `etc2`, `s3tc` and others [2]; this build is `nothreads` [23]. The docs are explicit that `OS.has_feature("mobile")` returns false for a web export running on a mobile device; check `web_android` or `web_ios` [2]. Custom feature tags are set in the export preset and "apply only to exported builds" [2]; `export_presets.cfg` has `custom_features=""` today. Project settings can be overridden per tag with the `setting.feature` key form (the project's own `rendering_method.mobile` is one), and `ProjectSettings.get_setting_with_override` is needed to read them [2]. Because custom tags are fixed at export, they distinguish builds, not devices: one web build serving many devices cannot use them for runtime tiers; they fit native per-store builds.

Adapter and display queries: `RenderingServer.get_video_adapter_type()` "always returns" `DEVICE_TYPE_OTHER` under the OpenGL driver [11], so it cannot separate integrated from discrete GPUs here; `get_video_adapter_name()` returns the driver string, which on web is whatever the browser exposes as the WebGL renderer (what Safari or Chrome on iPad return was not verified); `get_current_rendering_method()` returns `gl_compatibility` [11]. `DisplayServer.screen_get_dpi()` and `screen_get_scale()` are implemented on Android, iOS and Web (72 and 1.0 elsewhere); `screen_get_refresh_rate()` is not implemented on Web and returns -1.0 [12]. `OS.get_memory_info()` returns a dictionary whose entries "may be -1 if unknown" [10]; `platform/web/os_web.cpp` has no override, and the base implementation returns -1 for all four keys [22][23], so memory is unknown on web. `OS.get_processor_count()` on web calls a JavaScript hardware-concurrency function (`godot_js_os_hw_concurrency_get`) [22]. Through `JavaScriptBridge.eval` (web only [51]; already used in `dev_scene.gd`) the page can read `navigator.deviceMemory`, which is coarse and clamped [38] and supported by Chrome 63+, Edge 79+ and Samsung Internet, not by Firefox or Safari [37]; `WEBGL_debug_renderer_info` may be unavailable or masked depending on browser privacy settings [39]. On an iPad every browser is WebKit (App Store guideline 2.5.6 requires WebKit for apps that browse the web, with an entitlement route for the EU and Japan) [41], so the Chrome-only signals are absent there.

Performance monitors [13][11]: `TIME_PROCESS`, `TIME_FPS`, `RENDER_TOTAL_DRAW_CALLS_IN_FRAME`, `RENDER_TOTAL_OBJECTS_IN_FRAME`, `RENDER_TEXTURE_MEM_USED` and `RENDER_VIDEO_MEM_USED` are available in release builds; `MEMORY_STATIC` and `OBJECT_ORPHAN_NODE_COUNT` are debug-only; `get_rendering_info()` returns 0 until two frames have rendered. The paper_look scene already prints FPS, draw calls and objects from these. A first-launch microbenchmark (render a fixed scene for N frames and read `TIME_PROCESS`) is the one signal that exists on every platform, which is why ticket 5d8 names it.

Resource packs [3][4][30][31]: `ProjectSettings.load_resource_pack(pack, replace_files = true, offset = 0)` loads a `.pck` or `.zip` into `res://`; a file at an existing path replaces it unless `replace_files` is false [4]. Packs must load before the resources they override are loaded: a pack loaded in a menu "will not affect the other scene that was already preloaded", so the docs say to load in an autoload's `_init()` [3]. `--export-pack <preset> <path>` exports only the pack, in PCK or ZIP by extension, with `--headless` required where there is no GPU [30]; export presets choose which resources to include (all, selected scenes, selected resources, all except) with include and exclude filters [31]. `--export-patch` produces delta packs for updates, not tiers [3][30]. There is no per-platform import parameter: import settings live in one `.import` file per asset with project-wide defaults [32]; the only per-platform texture choice the engine makes is the VRAM-compressed format family [4][5]. Tiers of different texture resolution therefore have to be different files, built from the same masters by the bake scripts, either as separate packs or as separate `res://` paths selected at runtime.

Texture import detection: "Detect 3D" switches a texture to VRAM Compressed with mipmaps when it is used in a 3D scene [5]; `detect_3d/compress_to=1` is VRAM Compressed. This never fires in a 2D project. The import dock's Preset menu and the Import Defaults tab set defaults per resource type [32]; preset names were not verified against the docs.

## 3. Web-specific constraints

Threads: since 4.3 the single-threaded export is the default [1]. Thread support needs `Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy: same-origin` and `Cross-Origin-Embedder-Policy: require-corp`, and without them "the project will not run" [1]; `_headers` sets neither and the preset has `thread_support=false`, which is consistent. The build's Emscripten flags set `INITIAL_MEMORY` 32 MB, `ALLOW_MEMORY_GROWTH=1`, `WASM_MEM_MAX=2048MB`, stack 5120 KB and `MAX_WEBGL_VERSION=2` [24]; pages are 64 KiB [52][53]. The engine's own ceiling is therefore 2 GiB of wasm heap; the browser's limit on a given tablet is lower and was not verified.

Audio playback type: `audio/general/default_playback_type.web` defaults to 1 (Sample), which "allows for lower latency on the web platform at the cost of flexibility"; `audio/driver/output_latency.web` defaults to 50 ms as a "Safer override ... to avoid audio issues especially on mobile devices"; `audio/driver/mix_rate.web` defaults to 0, "let the browser choose" [4]. Sample mode "prevents the audio from being garbled in single-threaded Web exports"; only the web uses samples by default; "Changing this setting is not recommended" [27]. In Sample mode `AudioEffects are not supported` [7], the reverb bus and Doppler do not work, and Stream mode works "at the cost of increased latency if threads are not enabled" [27][1]. The v0.2.1 hotfix removed the Clones bus PitchShift and Distortion and baked the voices offline (commit 3008596), which is the correct response to this limitation. What Sample mode costs: `AudioDriverWeb::register_sample` instantiates a playback, allocates `frames_total = mix_rate * length` frames, calls `mix(frames_ptr, 1.0f, frames_total)` once over the whole stream, copies to a planar buffer of `frames_total * 2` floats and hands it to `godot_audio_sample_register_stream` [26]. For `island_night.mp3` at 176.40 s and a 44.1 kHz mix rate that is 44,100 x 176.40 x 2 x 4 B = 62.2 MB of float PCM decoded synchronously on the main thread the first time the track is registered; the JavaScript side's retained copy was not read and is unverified. The music crossfade keeps two players live, so two tracks can be registered in the same window.

Autoplay: the Godot docs say some browsers restrict autoplay and advise a click, tap or key on a splash screen [1]. Chrome: "Muted autoplay is always allowed"; autoplay with sound requires that "The user has interacted with the domain (click, tap, etc.)"; an AudioContext created before a gesture "will be created in the 'suspended' state" and needs `resume()` after the gesture; the policy shipped in Chrome 66 for media and Chrome 71 for Web Audio [34]. iOS WebKit: video autoplays without a gesture only "if their source media contains no audio tracks" or is muted; a gesture is a call that "must have directly resulted from a handler for a touchend, click, doubleclick, or keydown event"; a video that gains audio without a gesture pauses [35]. The intro's `play()` in `_ready()` with a Vorbis track is exactly the case both policies block, which matches tickets hdg (video waits) and 1w3 (picture ahead of sound). Video decoding is CPU-only ("GPUs don't have hardware acceleration for decoding Theora videos") and the docs say "low-end mobile CPUs will likely struggle with high-resolution videos"; 720p at most for mobile and web [29].

Background tabs: `requestAnimationFrame()` calls "are paused in most browsers when running in background tabs" [40] and the Godot docs say the project is paused when its tab is inactive [1]. The 89f Night measurement is flagged in the ticket as possibly taken from a background tab; SOP-12 now requires `document.visibilityState === "visible" && document.hasFocus()` recorded with every frame-timing probe.

WebGL2 and textures: Safari "has several issues with WebGL 2.0 support that other browsers don't have" and the docs recommend Chromium or Firefox [1]; on iPad that recommendation has no effect because every browser there is WebKit [41]. Texture size limits are in section 1 [36][50].

Lazy resource packs on web: the engine's loader resolves `mainPack` from the page config (`this.config.mainPack || exe + ".pck"`), passes `--main-pack`, and runs `Promise.all([this.init(exe), this.preloadFile(pack, pack)])` before `start`, copying preloaded files into the in-memory filesystem with `copyToFS` [25]. The whole main pack is downloaded and resident in wasm memory before the first frame. Two documented pieces allow a later fetch: `HTTPRequest.download_file` writes a response body to a path [21] (HTTP on web "cannot progress more than once per frame" [1]), `user://` persists through IndexedDB when the browser allows it [1][22], and `load_resource_pack` reads a `.pck` from a path [4]. No documentation addresses `load_resource_pack` on web specifically; treat it as a spike (H12). A custom HTML shell (`html/custom_html_shell`, empty today) can choose `mainPack` per tier before the engine starts, with no engine change; `site/src/worker.js` matches only `index.wasm|index.pck` and would need the per-tier names.

## 4. Pre-render instead of compute, concretely

Baked edges and shadows (done, column C). The generator pads each frame by `pad = ceil(maxEdge*s + 3*blur*s) + 2` texture px so border and shadow never spill into the neighbour, then writes one edge sheet per (edge width, paper) and two shadow sheets [tools/gen_paper_edges.py]. Sheet growth per frame is `(fw + 2*pad) * (fh + 2*pad) / (fw * fh)`; measured for npc_mother at s = 4: 192 x 272 / (128 x 208) = 1.96x. Shipping one chosen edge sheet and one shadow sheet per subject is two textures where A had one; because the shadow and the main sprite are different textures and y-sort interleaves subjects, C costs up to two batches per subject against A's one. Putting a subject's shadow frames and edge frames into the same sheet (one texture, two regions) removes that split under the rule in section 1 [16]. The B column's cost is structural: nine extra quads per subject, each with a `ShaderMaterial`, so nine batch breaks per subject [16]; no amount of tuning makes B cheaper than C.

Baked tints versus CanvasModulate and Light2D. A per-phase tint is one uniform multiply [16][18]; pre-rendering it costs `T` phases x every world texture for zero frame-time gain. Do not bake tints. Lights are different: a lit pixel pays one light-texture read per light plus 1, 5 or 13 shadow-atlas reads [18], and each shadow light adds an occluder pass [16]; a baked shadow is one textured quad that batches with its neighbours when it shares their texture. Baked shadows are the right call for the paper look; they fix the light direction per bake, which is acceptable for an overhead cut-out world (the sidecar already lets the engine set the shadow's offset and strength per scene).

Pre-rendered animation frames versus shader effects. A shader on a sprite is a material change and a batch break [16], and on WebGL the first use of a shader variant compiles it at runtime (the Compatibility limit `max_renderable_elements` is documented as reducing "shader compile times, especially on web" [4]; the `PIPELINE_COMPILATIONS_CANVAS` monitor counts "pipeline compilations that were triggered by the 2D canvas renderer" [13], whether it counts under GL was not verified). The tap pixelate (`shaders/pixelate.gdshader`) is assigned only while the effect runs, so idle frames pay nothing; the first tap pays the compile. The baked alternative is `F` extra frames per subject at the sheet's frame size, which for a 0.5 s effect at the rig's 8 fps is small, but it replaces a continuous tween with discrete steps. For the studio intro, a frame sheet is not viable: 7 s x 24 fps = 168 frames x 640 x 360 x 4 B = 155 MB uncompressed, so pre-rendering there means decomposing the animation into moving parts, not storing frames.

Atlases and batching. "Packing many smaller textures into a singular large texture helps to optimize video memory costs and render calls" [15]; `AtlasTexture` cannot be used in `AnimatedTexture` and does not tile [15]. `RigSheet.frames()` already builds `SpriteFrames` from `AtlasTexture` regions of one sheet, so a rig animates inside one texture; the 17 canon prop textures in the village do not share a texture and break batches wherever y-sort interleaves them. The `TextureAtlas` import type "can be used to reduce memory usage for animated 2D sprites" and is 2D-only [5]; an offline packer writing one 4096-wide atlas plus a region JSON (the pattern `island_tiles.json` already uses) is the equivalent under the project's existing tooling.

Resolution tiers and the formulas. Resident texture bytes for RGBA8 are `W x H x 4`, times 4/3 with mipmaps [50][5]; with VRAM compression `W x H x 1` (DXT5, ETC2 RGBA8, ASTC 4x4) or `W x H x 0.5` (DXT1, ETC2 RGB) [47][48][49]. `npc_mother_rig.png` as shipped: 2360 x 1440 x 4 x 4/3 = 18.1 MB. The C bake at s = 4: 1536 x 816 x 4 x 4/3 = 6.7 MB, and 5.0 MB without mipmaps. The texture resolution a device needs is `s_needed = (W_screen / 640) x camera_zoom` texture px per world px for `canvas_items` stretch, because that mode renders "directly at the target resolution" [33]; example input `W_screen = 2048` gives s = 6.4, so the source's 9.2 px per world px is 1.4x oversampled there and the 4x bake is 1.6x undersampled, while at the 1280 px window the source is 2.3x oversampled. The tier knob is `--scale` in `gen_paper_edges.py`, already a parameter. The `viewport` stretch mode renders at the base size and scales the result, and dividing it by Stretch Scale "reduces rendering resolution ... which can improve performance" [33]; the root Window exposes it as the `content_scale_mode` property (`CONTENT_SCALE_MODE_VIEWPORT`: "rendered at the base size and then scaled to the target size") with `content_scale_factor` beside it [54], which is the per-tier switch for a GPU-bound tablet at the cost of the crispness `canvas_items` gives; the docs carry no note on changing it after startup, so item 8 verifies that on the device.

Audio tiers. File size is `bitrate x seconds / 8`: 112 kbps x 176.4 s / 8 = 2.47 MB, which matches the file. Sample-mode memory is PCM and independent of bit rate: `mix_rate x seconds x 2 x 4 B` [26], with two channels fixed because the driver writes left and right unconditionally, so a mono source is duplicated; the lever there is duration, not bit rate or channel count. MP3 "requires less CPU usage to play back compared to Ogg Vorbis", WAV is cheap, Quite OK Audio is "much lower than MP3" [28]; in Sample mode the browser plays the decoded buffer, so per-frame decode cost is replaced by the one-time decode above.

## 5. Rare and Donkey Kong Country: what transfers

Rare bought SGI Challenge workstations with Alias rendering software around 1992 at about 80,000 pounds each, modelled characters in NURBS in PowerAnimator, rendered them, and reduced the renders into SNES sprites with a compression step they called Advanced Computer Modelling; "A single SGI screen took up more memory than an entire SNES cartridge", and Gregg Mayles called splitting backgrounds into tiles "the bane of the project" [42]. Steve Mayles: "If I handed Chris Sutherland a disk with a 16-frame walk animation, he'd probably only use every other frame," "That had to be done to fit it in the allotted space"; Sutherland described cutting a pre-rendered screen into squares and finding "places where pieces of the image could be repeated elsewhere to save video [memory] space" [43]. The hardware they fit into: sprites are 4 bpp 16-colour tiles choosing one of 8 palettes, 128 sprites in OAM, 32 per scanline [44]; 4 bpp pixels index 0-15 and pixel value 0 is transparent [45]; CGRAM holds 256 15-bit colours from 32,768, and VRAM is 64 KB [46]. How Rare performed the colour reduction and how much hand correction followed is not established by any source read for this review; treat accounts of "15 colours" as unverified.

What transfers: render once offline at any cost, ship the result, and let the target's fixed budget decide the frame count, resolution and variant count; frame dropping as a tier knob (every other frame) maps directly onto `RigSheet`'s idle and walk frame counts; Sutherland's tile reuse is atlas deduplication; the cartridge limit maps onto Cloudflare's 25 MiB per-file cap and the download the player pays before the first frame. What does not transfer: palette arithmetic and per-pixel touch-up (the GPU samples RGBA8 or block-compressed textures and the engine filters them), the fixed sprite and scanline limits, and the assumption of one target. Their constraint was one console; ours is a spread of devices, which is why detection and tiers replace their single budget.

## Action points for the dev team to test

Each item is a hypothesis with the change, the measurement, the pass criterion, the asset or build cost, and the ticket.

1. The tablet has no frame-time datum; take it before sizing anything. Change: nothing. Measure: open `?scene=paper_look` and the village on the iPad, read the overlay (FPS, draw calls, objects) and add `Performance.TIME_PROCESS` and `RENDER_TEXTURE_MEM_USED` to the readout [13]; record `visibilityState` and `hasFocus()` with the numbers (SOP-12). Pass: a recorded idle and walking frame time for Morning and Night on the tablet. Cost: none. Ticket: 5d8 (first datum), nmz.

2. The Night slowdown is not rendering. Change: nothing until re-measured. Measure: repeat 89f's Night idle with the focus probe asserted; if Night idle still exceeds Morning idle, repeat with `Music` muted and then with the phase forced without a track change, since the only code paths that change at night are the HUD label, the music track and NPC schedule positions (no Light2D, CanvasModulate or shader exists in the village). Pass: Night idle median within 1 ms of Morning idle median over 20 s; otherwise the responsible path is isolated. Cost: none. Ticket: 89f.

3. Sample-mode registration of a music track causes a single long frame at the phase change. Change: log frame time around `Music.play()`; then pre-register tracks at map load (play at `volume_db` -80 and stop) or on the chapter card. Measure: the maximum single-frame time at the Morning-to-Night switch in the village on Iris Xe Chrome and on the tablet, before and after. Pass: no frame over 33 ms at the switch. Cost: none for pre-registration; if duration is cut to lower the 62 MB PCM footprint per track, re-render the loops shorter. Ticket: nmz, 89f.

4. C (baked) is the production path and its draw calls equal A's when edge and shadow share one sheet. Change: extend `gen_paper_edges.py` to write the chosen edge sheet and the shadow into one texture (two row blocks) and have paper_look's C column read both from it. Measure: `RENDER_TOTAL_DRAW_CALLS_IN_FRAME` with only column A shown, then only C, on the tablet and on Iris Xe. Pass: C's draw calls <= A's draw calls + the number of subjects whose neighbours differ in texture (equal when the shadow shares the texture), and C's frame time within 1 ms of A's. Cost: per subject one sheet of `1.96 x 2` frame area at s = 4 (measured pad), replacing the eight test sheets. Ticket: 1dx, gsd.

5. A low tier at s = 2 and a high tier at s = 4 or more, baked from the same masters. Change: run the bake at `--scale 2` and `--scale 4` (and 8 for the high tier if the tablet's `s_needed` exceeds 4) for every rig and prop that ships with edges. Measure: `RENDER_TEXTURE_MEM_USED` in the village per tier, and visual review on the tablet at its real `s_needed = (W_screen / 640) x 2`. Pass: texture memory for the low tier under the budget set from the tablet's measurement in item 1, and no visible aliasing at the device's scale for the tier assigned to it. Cost: per sheet `W x H x 4 x 4/3` bytes resident and the WebP-compressed size in the pack; the s = 2 sheet is one quarter of the s = 4 sheet. Ticket: 5d8.

6. Nearest filtering with generated mipmaps wastes a third of texture memory. Change: either set the low tier's filter to Linear Mipmap (project setting override or per tier at launch) or disable `mipmaps/generate` on sprites that stay Nearest. Measure: `RENDER_TEXTURE_MEM_USED` before and after; the paper_look filter toggle for the look. Pass: memory down by about a quarter with mipmaps off, or the owner accepts the Linear Mipmap look on the tablet. The reason for the hotfix's return to Nearest is not recorded in commit 3008596 and must be asked. Cost: a re-import, no new assets. Ticket: 5d8, gsd.

7. One prop atlas cuts village draw calls. Change: pack the 17 canon prop textures into one atlas (4096 px wide at most, the limit the tile atlas already respects) with a region JSON, and build the village's Sprite2D nodes from `AtlasTexture` regions. Measure: `RENDER_TOTAL_DRAW_CALLS_IN_FRAME` idle and walking in the village, before and after, on the tablet. Pass: draw calls fall by at least the number of prop-to-prop texture alternations y-sort produced before (count from the before run); walking frame time improves or holds. Cost: one atlas texture of the summed prop area plus padding; no change to per-prop VRAM. Ticket: nmz, 5d8.

## What we do not know yet

- No frame-time measurement exists on a tablet yet; every tier size above is unbounded until item 1 runs.
- Whether 89f's 32.8 ms Night idle was a background-tab reading (the ticket's own comment says so; the probe rule is now in SOP-12).
- Why the v0.2.1 hotfix returned to Nearest filtering; the commit records the change and not the cause.
- What `RenderingServer.get_video_adapter_name()` returns under WebGL on the iPad, and whether the browser masks it.
- Whether `load_resource_pack` works from `user://` on the web export; the docs are silent.
- What the browser retains after `godot_audio_sample_register_stream` (the JavaScript side was not read), which mix rate the iPad's browser picks with `mix_rate.web = 0`, and how long the synchronous decode of a 176 s MP3 takes on a tablet.
- Whether `PIPELINE_COMPILATIONS_*` monitors count under the GL driver.
- The browser's wasm memory ceiling on the target tablets against the engine's 2 GiB `WASM_MEM_MAX`, and scene load time for the Lossless WebP sheets on a tablet CPU.
- The device matrix: which tablets and phones, and their screen widths, which fix `s_needed` per tier.
- Whether what the owner sees on the tablet is engine cost or bugs; items 1 and 2 are designed to tell them apart before any asset is re-baked.
